# **ResolveFlow AI — Policy-Grounded Customer Care Agent with Memory, Tools, and Audit Trail**

## 1. Project Title

**ResolveFlow AI: A Transaction-Grade Customer Care Agent That Resolves, Not Just Replies**

---

## 2. One-Line Pitch

**ResolveFlow AI is a customer-care agent that remembers the customer, checks company policy, calls backend tools, resolves complex issues, and produces an auditable proof trail for every action.**

---

## 3. Core Story

Most AI support bots today are still **answer bots**. They can reply to FAQs, summarize policy pages, and sometimes collect basic details. But real customer care is not just answering.

Real customer care means:

```text
Customer has a billing issue.
Customer is angry.
Customer also has service outage.
Customer wants cancellation.
Company policy has conditions.
Backend account data must be checked.
A credit/refund may or may not be allowed.
A human may need to join.
The customer should not repeat everything.
```

This is where most bots fail.

**ResolveFlow AI** is designed for this exact gap. It is not another FAQ chatbot. It is a **resolution engine** for customer care.

Your uploaded research strategy correctly says FlowZint is rewarding specialized AI agents, not generic app wrappers, with emphasis on innovation, real-world applicability, technical architecture, and documentation clarity. It also identifies the strongest product gap as an **integrated, transaction-grade resolution agent** that combines memory, policy grounding, tool use, user coordination, and proof that rules were followed. 

---

## 4. Final Problem Statement

Customer support bots fail because they are optimized for **conversation**, not **resolution**.

They usually cannot:

1. Remember prior customer history.
2. Handle multiple issues in one conversation.
3. Follow business policies strictly.
4. Use backend tools reliably.
5. Decide when to clarify, act, or escalate.
6. Produce proof that the action was policy-compliant.
7. Transfer context to a human without making the customer repeat everything.

ResolveFlow AI solves this by combining:

```text
Memory + Policy RAG + Tool Calling + Clarification + Handoff + Audit Trail
```

# 8. Full Feature List

## A. Core MVP Features

### 1. Multi-Issue Intent Detection

The agent detects when the customer has more than one issue.

Example:

```text
"I was charged twice and my internet is not working."
```

Detected as:

```text
Billing dispute + Service outage
```

Why it matters: real customers do not speak in clean single-intent queries.

---

### 2. Customer Memory Layer

ResolveFlow stores and retrieves customer history.

Memory types:

```text
Stable memory:
- customer plan
- preferred language
- long-term preferences

Episodic memory:
- previous complaints
- previous tickets
- prior credits/refunds

Recent session memory:
- what customer already said in this chat
- what tools were already called
- what clarification was already asked
```

Research basis: LongMemEval shows long-term assistant memory requires information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, and abstention; it also reports large accuracy drops in sustained-interaction memory settings. ([arXiv][1])

---

### 3. Policy-Grounded Retrieval

The bot retrieves policy before answering risky questions.

Example policies:

```text
Refund policy
Service credit policy
Plan downgrade policy
Cancellation policy
Technician visit policy
Duplicate payment policy
```

Every final answer should cite internally which policy was used.

Research basis: Self-RAG argues retrieval should be adaptive and reflection-based rather than always stuffing fixed passages into context. ([arXiv][2]) CRAG adds a retrieval evaluator that checks document quality and triggers corrective behavior when retrieval is weak. ([arXiv][3])

---

### 4. Policy Graph / Policy DAG

Instead of letting the LLM freely decide, high-risk actions pass through a policy graph.

Example:

```text
Apply service credit?
    ↓
Was outage verified?
    ↓
Was outage > 6 hours?
    ↓
Has credit already been applied this cycle?
    ↓
Credit amount allowed?
    ↓
Apply credit / escalate
```

Research basis: JourneyBench shows customer-support agents need structured policy-aware orchestration, and its Dynamic-Prompt Agent improved policy adherence enough that a smaller model could outperform a larger one in that setup. ([arXiv][4]) SOP-Bench also shows LLM agents struggle with complex SOP-following, with low average success rates in industrial workflows. ([arXiv][5])

---

### 5. Tool-Calling Layer

ResolveFlow uses explicit backend tools instead of pretending to know account state.

MVP tools:

```text
lookup_customer(customer_id)
get_invoice_history(customer_id)
check_duplicate_charge(customer_id)
check_outage_status(location)
run_router_diagnostic(customer_id)
retrieve_policy(policy_name)
apply_credit(customer_id, amount, reason)
create_ticket(customer_id, issue_type)
schedule_technician(customer_id, slot)
change_plan(customer_id, plan_id)
generate_handoff_summary(conversation_id)
```

Research basis: τ-bench is built around realistic tool-agent-user interaction with domain-specific APIs and policy guidelines, and it found even strong function-calling agents can be inconsistent in real-world domains. ([arXiv][6]) Also, API-based agents outperform browser-only agents when APIs are available, making explicit tools the right hackathon engineering choice. ([arXiv][7])

---

### 6. Clarification Engine

The bot asks targeted clarification only when required.

Bad bot:

```text
Can you provide more details?
```

ResolveFlow:

```text
Was this charge on your broadband bill or your mobile add-on subscription?
```

This feature comes from our earlier **ClariBridge** idea.

Decision options:

```text
ANSWER
ASK_CLARIFICATION
CALL_TOOL
APPLY_POLICY
HANDOFF
```

---

### 7. Conversation Health Score

The system tracks whether the conversation is becoming risky.

Formula:

```text
Conversation Health Score =
30% intent confidence
25% missing information risk
20% sentiment / frustration
15% repeated-question loop
10% knowledge coverage
```

Example:

```text
Intent confidence: 72
Missing information risk: Medium
Sentiment: Negative
Loop detected: No
Knowledge coverage: Low

Health Score: 46/100
Action: Ask clarification or prepare handoff
```

This comes from our **ClariBridge + MirrorMind** discussion.

---

### 8. Warm Human Handoff

When the agent cannot safely resolve, it escalates before failure.

Handoff trigger examples:

```text
Policy exception required
Customer anger rising
Repeated question loop detected
Knowledge coverage too low
High-value customer at churn risk
Tool failure
Refund above allowed threshold
```

# 15. Agent Decision Logic

Use this constrained flow:

```text
1. Classify customer intent.
2. Detect whether multiple issues exist.
3. Retrieve customer memory.
4. Retrieve relevant policy.
5. Check retrieval confidence.
6. Identify missing slots.
7. If missing critical info → ask clarification.
8. If enough info → call required tools.
9. Validate proposed action against policy graph.
10. Execute allowed action.
11. Generate response.
12. Save audit trail.
13. If unsafe / unsupported → warm handoff.
```

Important: avoid unrestricted autonomous loops. Judges will trust a constrained architecture more.
